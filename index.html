    About the dates

    Dates follow a traditional chronology that takes the Bible’s own numbers at face value: an Exodus around 1446 BC (1 Kings 6:1) and the widely used reign dates worked out by Edwin Thiele for the kings. Many historians place the Exodus near 1260 BC and the patriarchs later. Reign dates can differ by a year or two because of shared reigns between fathers and sons and different calendars. Where scholars propose clearly different dates, a thin line under the entry shows the range.

    Genesis 1–11 is placed by adding up the ages in Genesis 5 and 11 (the Hebrew text) and counting back from Abraham. That puts creation at about 4174 BC and the Flood at about 2518 BC. Archbishop Ussher’s well-known 4004 BC uses the same method with a later date for Abraham, while the Greek Septuagint’s numbers add roughly 1,400 years. Many Bible scholars read these genealogies as selective rather than complete, and geology and astronomy date the earth and universe far older. This section therefore shows the Bible’s own reckoning, not dates historians agree on. On these numbers the Flood falls within Egypt’s Old Kingdom, whose records show no break.

    Dates for Egypt and Mesopotamia follow the standard chronology. On those dates the 1446 BC Exodus falls under Thutmose III; those who hold the early Exodus usually place Egypt’s kings a few decades earlier, making Amenhotep II the pharaoh of the Exodus. Anything before about 900 BC is approximate, and judges are shown by their time in office.
